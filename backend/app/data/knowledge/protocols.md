# SECTION 1: EMERGENCY SERVICES

## 1. Police (Law Enforcement)

**Role**: Primary law enforcement agency responsible for maintaining public order, preventing and detecting crime, protecting life and property, and enforcing laws and regulations.

**Priority**: 1 (Critical Life Threat) to 2 (Emergency Response Required)

**Keywords**: crime, theft, robbery, burglary, assault, murder, homicide, shooting, stabbing, rape, kidnapping, abduction, domestic violence, fight, brawl, gang activity, weapon, gun, knife, threat, suspicious person, trespassing, vandalism, breaking and entering, looting, fraud, scam, extortion, harassment, stalking, missing person, fugitive, warrant, arrest, lockdown, active shooter, hostage, ransom, carjacking, mugging, pickpocket, mob, riot, violence, attack, injured officer, officer down, backup needed, pursuit, high-speed chase, barricaded suspect, armed suspect, intruder, prowler, peeping tom, indecent exposure, public disturbance, noise complaint escalated, civil dispute violent, neighbor dispute violent, property dispute violent, breach of peace

**Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)**:
- Immediately obtain caller's exact location and callback number
- Determine if situation is active/ongoing or has concluded
- Assess immediate threat to life (weapons present, violence occurring, injuries)
- Identify number of suspects, physical descriptions, direction of travel
- Determine if suspects are armed and type of weapon
- Ascertain number of victims/witnesses present
- Check for any hostages or barricaded situations
- Dispatch nearest available patrol units (minimum 2 units for violent crimes)
- Alert supervisor for Priority 1 calls
- Notify detective division if crime has concluded and scene is secure
- Update responding units with real-time information from caller
- Coordinate backup units for high-risk situations
- Notify SWAT/tactical units if armed standoff or hostage situation
- Keep caller on line if safe to do so
- Advise caller on immediate safety actions (lock doors, hide, leave area)
- Document all caller statements verbatim
- Record incident number and assigned units
- Monitor unit status and provide updates
- Coordinate with other agencies if needed (Fire/EMS if injuries present)

## 2. Fire & Rescue

**Role**: Emergency response service for fire suppression, rescue operations, hazardous materials incidents, and other life-threatening emergencies involving fire, smoke, or structural hazards.

**Priority**: 1 (Critical Life Threat - active fire with trapped persons) to 2 (Emergency Response Required - fire/rescue needed)

**Keywords**: fire, smoke, flames, burning, blaze, inferno, structure fire, house fire, building fire, apartment fire, office fire, factory fire, warehouse fire, vehicle fire, car fire, truck fire, forest fire, wildfire, brush fire, grass fire, electrical fire, gas fire, oil fire, chemical fire, explosion, blast, detonation, people trapped, person trapped, cannot escape, stuck, pinned, collapsed building, structural collapse, cave-in, trench collapse, confined space, elevator stuck, rescue needed, smoke inhalation, burns, fire alarm, smoke detector, sprinkler activated, smell of smoke, smell of gas, propane leak, natural gas leak, fuel spill, hazmat, hazardous materials, toxic fumes, chemical spill, industrial accident, scaffolding collapse, construction accident, high-angle rescue, rope rescue, water rescue, ice rescue, technical rescue, extrication, jaws of life needed, airplane crash, train derailment, smoke visible, flames visible, fully involved, working fire

**Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)**:
- Obtain exact address and nearest cross streets immediately
- Determine type of structure (residential, commercial, industrial, high-rise)
- Identify if fire is active and location within structure
- Assess if anyone is trapped or unable to evacuate
- Determine number of occupants and if all accounted for
- Identify presence of smoke, flames, or both
- Ascertain if building is occupied or vacant
- Check for hazardous materials or special risks (gas stations, chemical plants)
- Dispatch appropriate apparatus (engine, ladder, rescue) based on fire type
- Send minimum first alarm assignment for structure fires
- Alert Battalion Chief for all working fires
- Notify EMS to stage for potential injuries
- Coordinate water supply for sustained operations
- Update responding units on fire conditions, trapped persons, exposures
- Advise caller to evacuate immediately if safe
- Instruct caller on closing doors to contain fire
- Keep caller on line if they cannot evacuate
- Request additional alarms if fire is spreading or multiple floors involved
- Notify utility companies for gas/electric shutoff if needed
- Alert neighboring fire departments for mutual aid if required
- Coordinate evacuation of adjacent buildings if necessary
- Document time of call, dispatch, and arrival times
- Establish command structure and incident command post
- Notify hazmat team if chemical/industrial fire
- Alert building department for structural concerns

## 3. Ambulance (Emergency Medical Services - EMS)

**Role**: Provide pre-hospital emergency medical care, patient stabilization, and rapid transport to appropriate medical facilities for individuals experiencing medical emergencies, traumatic injuries, or life-threatening conditions.

**Priority**: 1 (Critical Life Threat - cardiac arrest, severe trauma, unresponsive) to 2 (Emergency Response Required - serious medical condition)

**Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)**:
- Obtain exact location and apartment/suite/room number if applicable
- Get callback number in case call is disconnected
- Determine chief complaint and patient's current condition
- Assess if patient is conscious, breathing, and has pulse
- Identify age and sex of patient
- Determine if patient is experiencing chest pain, difficulty breathing, or severe bleeding
- Check for any known allergies or medical conditions
- Ascertain if patient takes any medications
- Identify if patient is diabetic, has heart condition, or seizure history
- Determine if incident is medical or trauma-related
- Dispatch appropriate level of care (BLS vs ALS) based on acuity
- Send closest available ambulance unit immediately for Priority 1 calls
- Provide pre-arrival instructions (CPR, bleeding control, positioning)
- Keep caller on line for Priority 1 life-threatening emergencies
- Guide caller through CPR if patient is in cardiac arrest
- Instruct on bleeding control if severe hemorrhage
- Advise on recovery position if unconscious but breathing
- Alert receiving hospital of incoming critical patient
- Notify police if traumatic injury may involve crime
- Notify fire department if extrication or lifting assistance needed
- Request air ambulance for remote locations or critical trauma
- Update responding units on patient condition changes
- Coordinate with specialty teams (hazmat, rescue) if needed
- Document all pre-arrival instructions given
- Monitor response times and unit availability
- Coordinate multiple units if mass casualty incident
- Alert trauma center if severe trauma patient
- Notify stroke center if stroke symptoms present
- Advise cardiac catheterization lab if STEMI suspected

## 5. Bomb Squad (Bomb Disposal Unit / Explosive Ordnance Disposal)

**Role**: Specialized unit trained to locate, identify, render safe, and dispose of explosive devices, improvised explosive devices (IEDs), unexploded ordnance, and suspicious packages that may contain explosives.

**Priority**: 1 (Critical Life Threat - confirmed explosive device or imminent threat) to 2 (Emergency Response Required - suspicious object requiring assessment)

**Keywords**: bomb, explosive, IED, improvised explosive device, suspicious package, suspicious object, suspicious bag, unattended luggage, bomb threat, explosion imminent, ticking sound, wires visible, device found, grenade, ordnance, unexploded bomb, unexploded ordnance, landmine, dynamite, TNT, C4, Semtex, pipe bomb, car bomb, vehicle bomb, package bomb, letter bomb, mail bomb, suicide bomber, explosive vest, backpack bomb, pressure cooker device, timer visible, detonator, blasting cap, suspicious powder, white powder, chemical smell strange, militarystyle equipment found, ammunition found, weapons cache, bomb factory, bombmaking materials, fertilizer large quantity, chemical precursors, triggering device, remote detonator, cell phone attached to device, suspicious vehicle, abandoned vehicle, vehicle smoking, terrorism, terrorist threat, domestic terrorism, explosive threat, credible threat, evacuation needed, building evacuated, area cordoned, robot needed, X-ray needed, render safe procedure

**Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)**:
- Immediately obtain exact location of suspicious item/device
- Determine description of object (size, shape, color, markings)
- Assess if object is attended or unattended
- Identify how long object has been in place
- Determine if anyone has touched or moved the object
- Ascertain if any threats were received (phone, email, note)
- Check for any unusual smells, sounds, or visible wires
- Identify number of people in immediate area
- Establish evacuation perimeter immediately (minimum 300 feet for small package)
- Dispatch bomb squad with all specialized equipment
- Notify police to establish outer perimeter and access control
- Alert fire department to stage at safe distance
- Notify EMS to stage at safe distance
- Evacuate all buildings within blast radius
- Stop all radio transmissions near suspected device
- Instruct caller and others NOT to use cell phones near device
- Prohibit anyone from approaching, touching, or moving object
- Establish command post outside danger zone
- Request bomb detection K-9 unit if available
- Deploy bomb disposal robot for initial assessment
- Arrange for x-ray imaging of suspicious object
- Coordinate with counter-terrorism units if credible threat
- Notify intelligence agencies if terrorism suspected
- Establish media staging area away from scene
- Shut down utilities in affected area if needed
- Coordinate traffic detours around evacuation zone
- Set up public information hotline for evacuees
- Document all actions and decisions
- Notify explosive disposal range if transport required
- Arrange for post-blast investigation team if detonation occurs
- Coordinate with FBI/ATF for federal investigation if needed

# SECTION 3: COMBINED RESPONSE PROTOCOLS (MULTI-SERVICE)

## FIRE-RELATED MULTI-SERVICE SCENARIOS

### 1. Structural Fire with Trapped Persons
**Trigger**: Fire in building/structure AND people unable to evacuate OR people reported trapped

**Primary Action**: Dispatch Fire & Rescue (Priority 1)
- Full first alarm assignment
- Multiple engines, ladder trucks, rescue units
- Battalion Chief
- Hazmat standby if commercial/industrial

**Secondary Action**: Dispatch Ambulance/EMS (Priority 1)
- Minimum 2 ALS ambulances
- Mass casualty bus if large building
- Burn center notification
- Stage at safe distance upwind

**Tertiary Action**: Alert Police (Priority 2)
- Perimeter control and crowd management
- Traffic control and road closures
- Evacuation assistance
- Investigation if suspicious origin

**Additional Coordination**:
- Utility companies for gas/electric shutoff
- Red Cross for displaced residents
- Building department for structural assessment
- Mental health crisis team for survivors

**Reason**: Fire poses immediate life threat requiring rapid fire suppression. Trapped persons need rescue and likely medical care. Police manage scene security and evacuation. Multi-agency response ensures all aspects of emergency are addressed simultaneously.

---

### 2. High-Rise Fire
**Trigger**: Fire in building over 7 stories OR high-rise building fire alarm

**Primary Action**: Dispatch Fire & Rescue (Priority 1)
- Minimum second alarm assignment automatically
- High-rise specialized equipment
- Multiple ladder companies
- Ventilation units
- Air supply support

**Secondary Action**: Dispatch Ambulance/EMS (Priority 1)
- Stage multiple ambulances at various levels
- Establish medical command post
- Coordinate with hospitals for capacity
- Prepare for extended operations

**Tertiary Action**: Alert Police (Priority 1)
- Large perimeter establishment
- Elevator control (civilians prevented from use)
- Stairwell access control
- Mass evacuation coordination

**Additional Coordination**:
- Building engineer/maintenance for building systems
- Utility companies for emergency shutdowns
- Transit authority if subway/public transport nearby
- Helicopter for roof rescues if available
- Building management for floor plans and occupancy

**Reason**: High-rise fires are extremely complex requiring massive resources. Evacuation is difficult, rescue operations challenging, and coordination critical. Fire can spread rapidly through shafts. Multiple agencies ensure comprehensive response across all floors and all aspects of emergency.

---

### 3. Wildfire/Forest Fire Approaching Residential Area
**Trigger**: Wildfire within 2 miles of populated area OR fire spreading toward homes

- Structure protection teams

- Shelter establishment
- Resource coordination
- Incident command support

- Weather Department for wind conditions

- Animal rescue teams for livestock/pets
- Transportation department for road closures

**Reason**: Wildfires spread rapidly and unpredictably. Mass evacuation required to save lives. Fire suppression alone insufficient - need coordinated evacuation, resource management, and security. Multiple agencies ensure no aspect overlooked and all residents evacuated safely.

# APPENDIX: PRIORITY LEVEL DEFINITIONS

## Priority 1: Critical Life Threat
Immediate danger to life requiring instant emergency response. Response time target: Under 5 minutes.

**Examples**: Cardiac arrest, severe trauma, active fire with trapped persons, active shooter, terrorist attack, building collapse with trapped victims, major chemical leak with casualties.

**Response Protocol**: Dispatch all required units immediately, alert hospitals/trauma centers, establish command structure, coordinate all responding agencies.

---

## Priority 2: Emergency Response Required
Serious emergency requiring urgent response but not immediately life-threatening. Response time target: Under 10 minutes.

**Examples**: Chest pain, difficulty breathing, assault without life-threatening injuries, fire without trapped persons, serious traffic accident, gas leak without immediate danger, burglary in progress.

**Response Protocol**: Dispatch appropriate units immediately, assess need for additional resources, coordinate as needed.

---

## Priority 3: Urgent Situation
Situation requiring prompt response to prevent escalation or provide needed assistance. Response time target: Under 30 minutes.

**Examples**: Minor injuries, property crimes completed, utility failures affecting many, minor hazmat situations contained, animal emergencies, traffic accidents without serious injuries.

**Response Protocol**: Dispatch appropriate units, monitor situation for escalation, coordinate additional resources if needed.

---

## Priority 4: Routine Service Call
Non-emergency situation requiring response but no immediate danger. Response time target: Under 2 hours.

**Examples**: Minor traffic violations, noise complaints, minor property damage, routine animal control, minor utility issues affecting few people, general complaints.

**Response Protocol**: Schedule response during normal operations, coordinate with appropriate department, document complaint.

---

## Priority 5: Informational / Scheduled Service
Non-urgent inquiry or service that can be scheduled. Response time target: Next business day or scheduled time.

**Examples**: General information requests, service appointments, routine permits, scheduled inspections, non-urgent complaints, follow-up calls.

**Response Protocol**: Provide information, schedule service call, route to appropriate department, document request.

---

## ESCALATION TRIGGERS

Any Priority 3, 4, or 5 call can be escalated to higher priority based on:

- **Life Threat Develops**: Immediately escalate to Priority 1
- **Violence or Weapons Introduced**: Escalate to Priority 1 or 2
- **Fire or Explosion**: Escalate to Priority 1
- **Hazardous Materials Involved**: Escalate to Priority 1 or 2
- **Multiple Casualties**: Escalate to Priority 1
